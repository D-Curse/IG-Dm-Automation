Data Deletion Instructions

Last updated: 10 October 2026

You can request deletion of information stored by DM-Automation whether you operate a connected Instagram account or interacted with a reel processed by the app.

1. Send a deletion request

Email dhruvbhatada@gmail.com with the subject DM-Automation Data Deletion Request. Include your Instagram username, whether you operate a connected account or are a commenter, and any reel/comment link that helps us identify the records. Do not include your password or access token.

2. Verification and removal

We may request proportionate verification to prevent unauthorized deletion. After verification, we will identify and remove the relevant personal information from the app's stored records and configuration where applicable, subject to any lawful retention requirement. Connected-account removal includes associated rules and stored automation history, and removing its token from the service. Commenter removal includes the relevant stored username, comment content, identifiers, and associated action records. We will respond without undue delay and within applicable legal deadlines, and confirm completion or explain any lawful exception.

Deletion is handled manually by the operator; this page is an instructions page, not an automated data-deletion callback. Relevant logs and operator-created backups must also be considered when fulfilling your request. Any legally required retained data will be restricted to that purpose.

3. Disconnect your account

If you connected an Instagram account, pause its automations and remove DM-Automation from Instagram or Meta's connected apps/website permissions settings. Setting names can vary. Revoking access prevents future API use but does not automatically delete the app's stored database records, so also send the request above.

4. Data held by Instagram

Deleting app records does not automatically delete comments or private messages already posted on Instagram, or information independently retained by Meta. Manage those through Instagram and Meta's own privacy tools. Deleting duplicate-protection records may allow an old comment to be processed again if its automation remains enabled; the operator must disable affected processing when fulfilling your request.
